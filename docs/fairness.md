# Fairness review: synthetic October 2026 build

**Question.** Are award outcomes different across regions, assessors, assessment types or claimant age/condition groups? These descriptive comparisons are prompts for review, not evidence of unfair treatment or a basis for individual decisions.

**Observed example.** Among closed, curated daily-living cases, North has a raw award rate of **87.65%** (`n=688`), while South has **75.82%** (`n=736`). North's synthetic case mix contains **68.60%** mental-health-category cases, against South's **20.79%**. The generator intentionally makes this category more likely to have higher daily-living activity scores. When both regions are weighted to the same pooled condition-category distribution, the rates become **80.81%** and **80.39%**. The conspicuous 11.83-point raw gap shrinks to 0.42 point. See `evidence/local_results.json` for all four regions.

**How to repeat in Snowflake.** Query `MART.COMPONENT_OUTCOMES` for closed daily-living cases; group by `region`, `assessment_type`, `condition_category`, and assessor ID. Compare each group's rate with counts and uncertainty intervals. For case-mix adjustment, compute within-condition rates and weight them by the same pooled condition distribution, as the local check does. `MART.FUNNEL` adjusts assessor daily-living rates for condition and assessment type and shows binomial 95% and 99.8% limits that widen at small caseloads. Small groups should be suppressed or pooled in external reports.

**Limitations.** This is fabricated data with a deliberately planted association. Condition categories are broad proxies; age, severity, geography, referral patterns, assessor allocation and other confounders are not controlled. Challenge and audit scores are synthetic. The standardization uses only one case-mix variable, and small assessor/type cells can be unstable. A point outside a funnel limit is a signal to inspect data quality, case assignment, sample size and clinical context with the QA lead. It is **not** evidence of bias or misconduct. We would not rank or sanction assessors, infer discrimination, or change claimant decisions from these charts.
